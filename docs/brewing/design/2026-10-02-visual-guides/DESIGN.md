## The decision

Use one illustrated step canvas with two entry intents. From **Brew**, prepare the selected recipe and follow its physical actions. From **Learn**, explore the recipe overview and any step without starting a session. The same action, equipment, quantity basis, safety, diagram and completion cue serve both. Reading controls change what is being viewed; brewing controls confirm what was physically done.

The default screen answers four questions, in this order:

1. **What do I do?** One short action title, such as “Pour to 240 g.”
2. **How does it look?** One close, specific illustration of placement, movement or the state to observe.
3. **How much, and when?** The relevant quantity is large; its basis is explicit. Timing appears only when it helps this action.
4. **How do I know I am done?** One visible, observable cue and a named physical confirmation.

Explanations, variants and evidence are a contextual disclosure. Safety and indispensable operating instructions stay visible. “Minimal text” means removing duplication and background explanation, not removing safe handling or changing a mass target into an unexplained number.

## Information architecture and actions

**Learn → method → recipe overview → any step.** The overview shows equipment identity, two correctly labelled recipe quantities, grind/temperature when useful, and a small visual outline. “Explore the steps” is primary. A reader can skip, backtrack, expand an explanation, view sources, leave and resume their reading position. Nothing creates a session, starts a clock, changes a recipe, or records physical completion. “Brew this” crosses into preparation only after the exact recipe and equipment are resolved.

**Brew → current setup → preparation → physical start → current step → finish.** “Prepare to brew” is primary in brew context; learning is secondary. Existing remembered setup supplies the equipment choices, with a contextual correction only if required. Guide creation is not extraction start. Preparation confirmations describe observable readiness; the first timed action names the physical event (“Start first pour”, “Pump has started”, “Refrigerated · start steep”).

**Within live brewing → Look ahead → Return to live.** A visible banner states that the brew continues. Previewed steps use reading navigation and cannot dispatch session mutations, including when the reader happens to view the current live stage. The live stage index and viewed stage index are separate. Returning restores the actual action and current physical elapsed time, not the preview position.

Default live action hierarchy:

- Dominant full-width bottom action: the current physical confirmation, such as “240 g total reached”, “Pump stopped”, “Drainage is finished”. This confirms the user's observation, not a sensor reading.
- During a source-defined wait: prominent elapsed/target relationship and the next physical action when ready. A deadline can change the cue to “Ready to release”; it cannot claim that a valve has opened.
- “Look ahead” in the step header. “Why this step?” below the visible instruction/cue/safety. Pause prompts and ending the session are secondary actions.
- No compulsory feedback form at finish. “Done” returns to the originating brewing context; notes/taste and “Brew again” are contextual. A fresh brew gets a new session ID and physical clock.

For hands-busy work, keep the confirmation in a stable location with a large target. Never require a swipe, precise drag, long press, timed tap or gesture-only control. The target and immediate safety line should remain readable at a glance; less urgent details can scroll. There is no automatic inference of pouring, pump state, valve position, refrigerator temperature or scale mass.

### The visual grammar

Preserve the app's three artwork roles. Brew/setup uses tintable `equipment_*.xml` silhouettes through `EquipmentVisual`/`EquipmentIcon`. Learn uses `learn_brewer_icon_*.webp` through `BrewerProfileIcon`, inside 68 dp tonal badges. The [reference-guided icon pack](../2026-10-02-method-icons/README.md) retains 17 equipment photographs and anatomy briefs. Revision two reuses ten shipping Learn images exactly and creates seven restrained extensions. The initial glossy generation is retained as rejected style evidence; anatomical and alpha checks alone did not establish visual consistency. These recognition icons do not replace Brew silhouettes or teaching illustrations.

Use actual accepted instructional art as the rendering reference. The older [illustration style guide](../../visual-style-guide.md) gives general composition constraints, but its description alone is insufficient to match the shipped artwork. This prototype retains seven compatible Chemex teaching images byte-for-byte and uses fifteen matching generated review scenes for its remaining steps. The built-in image generator received the shipping pouring scene as the style reference and equipment photographs for anatomy. The [gallery](artwork-review.html) and [exact prompts, dimensions and hashes](ILLUSTRATIONS.json) make the candidates reviewable. They are not approved production scenes. Preserve text-free, number-free, logo-free artwork, meaningful alt text and stable support; quantities and labels live in localized UI outside the image. Production remains approved WebP under the present ADR, with truthful text fallback where permitted when approved art is unavailable.

- **Placement:** the filter seam, valve, vessel contact, basket seating or grip is large enough to inspect. Crop to the relevant joint rather than shrinking the whole appliance.
- **Action:** one directional arrow or clear change of pose shows the specific pour, stir, press or turn. Direction does not depend only on color. No decorative flower animation competes with instruction.
- **Observation:** show the state being sought: wetted bed, safe headroom, no standing pool, occasional drips, rising foam, ready indicator, pressure/flow settled. A static diagram must still explain it.
- **Boundary:** do not draw liquid through a closed valve, stirring through a sealed lid, an unheld unstable stack, hot glass touched by fingers, or a number on an unconnected scale. Do not imply a diagram is a measured live liquid level.
- **Technique detail:** a reader may expand a close-up for the same action; this never becomes a timed mini-game. Critical geometry requires a reviewed visual rather than an attractive generic silhouette.

## Time, interruptions and navigation

**Preparation is untimed.** Creating or viewing a session does not establish first water, heat-on or pump-start. User confirmation of that specific event supplies the physical clock origin. Keep the first-pour clock shared with bloom and downstream reference cues; selecting a step, reopening the app or looking ahead never restarts it.

**Pause the guide, not extraction.** Separate prompt suspension from physical time. The UI says “Coffee is still extracting. The clock continues.” Preserve the current physical stage and timestamp; suspend routine prompt delivery, with an explicit policy for indispensable safety/deadline information. Do not repurpose the current frozen-active-time Pause semantics. Native notification policy and any old paused-session migration require deliberate implementation and tests. A future manual machine stop is an observed event, not an app control.

**Late entry:** offer “Already started?” only where a physical clock can begin. If the start is known, record the actual local timestamp or elapsed time with an explicit user-entered provenance; retain the original clock. If unknown, display “Time unknown” and use physical cues; do not invent precision. For a time-critical steep whose history is unknown, explain the missing timing and let the user choose a deliberate recovery/end action. Never auto-complete earlier pours or valve actions by comparing the clock to a schedule. The prototype accepts known elapsed seconds or “I don’t know the time”: manual/yield completion remains usable; an unknown timed wait offers start-time correction or explicit end. A native date/time editor and more detailed recovery still need implementation.

**Returning late:** reconcile physical elapsed time; show the current required action and the specific missed cue, without a backlog of alarms or negative countdown. If the user did the action while away, provide a contextual correction with its actual time when known; do not mark it as performed at app reopening. Timed readiness alone never confirms drainage, a press or a switched-off pump. For cold brew, show the expected local ready time plus elapsed duration and timezone-aware date in native UI; past-due means time to filter, not certified safe coffee.

**Lifecycle:** persist the compiled recipe/version/equipment snapshot, physical event timestamps, confirmations, measurements and prompt state. Persist reading position separately by recipe/version. Foreground uses monotonic deltas; restoration reconciles persisted wall anchors, handles wall/timezone changes deliberately and reports uncertainty instead of silently shortening extraction. Preserve the existing coordinator/outbox and idempotent log completion. Process death, rotation, lock screen and backgrounding are not requests to cancel or pause extraction.

**Back:** in a reader, return to the overview/library while remembering the step. During a brew, navigation can leave the guide while the durable session continues; its resume entry remains visible in brewing context. Do not discard a live session or convert the reader's viewed index into physical progress. Only an explicit end action terminates the session; confirmation is justified when it would abandon an active brew. The prototype permits one cold batch alongside a foreground brew and offers Resume before replacing either. Do not navigate back into editable preparation for a running snapshot and silently change its quantities.

The browser prototype uses a versioned localStorage snapshot for one cold batch and one foreground brew, plus wall time for review convenience. It restores simulated sessions after reload but has no transactional store, monotonic-clock reconciliation, multi-tab coordination or background notification delivery. These are explicit prototype limits. The [cold-brew reminder extension](COLD-BREW-REMINDERS.md) defines quick entry, custom durations, leave-and-return behavior and the native integration contract.

## Accessibility and adaptation

The following are externally supported constraints: Android recommends at least **48 × 48 dp** touch targets and meaningful graphic descriptions; native semantic components should be used and tested rather than assuming a custom canvas is accessible. [Android accessibility defaults](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/accessibility/api-defaults). Content should reflow at a narrow effective width rather than require two-dimensional reading; use 320 CSS px as a browser review condition. [W3C reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html). Interaction-triggered movement should be suppressible when it is not essential. [W3C animation from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html).

The layout decisions below are design judgments to validate natively:

- Use 56–64 dp primary actions, at least 48 dp secondary controls, ample spacing and purpose-based names. Numeric UI uses tabular figures. Unit and quantity-basis labels remain attached at every text size.
- At narrow width or large text, prioritize action, quantity, immediate safety, cue and primary action. Let the page scroll; reduce the illustration's height before truncating essential text. Move the bottom action into normal flow when a sticky bar would consume most of the viewport. Never hide warnings behind the illustration or crop captions.
- At wider width, keep one current action; place its diagram beside the instruction only when reading/focus order remains coherent. Avoid a desktop dashboard of every clock and stage.
- Screen-reader order: progress/context → title → quantity and basis → useful illustration description → instruction → cue → safety → timing → explanation → action. Suppress redundant decorative descriptions. Announce stage changes once; do not announce elapsed seconds continuously. Urgent safety status should not steal focus from a physical confirmation.
- Keyboard, Switch Access and TalkBack must reach all reading, explanation, start, confirmation, pause/resume and recovery actions. Move focus predictably to the new step heading; return dialog focus to its trigger. A repeated tap must not complete two physical stages.
- Static art communicates the whole action; optional movement can only clarify direction. With reduced motion, remove transitions/loops without losing a cue. The prototype is static art with a brief page transition only when reduced motion is not requested.
- Text/controls require measured contrast in final light/dark themes; color alone cannot distinguish active, completed, unsafe or waiting. Test 200% font scale, long localized strings and landscape/keyboard occlusion, not only an English portrait screenshot.

## Acceptance before native integration is called complete

- **Content:** all 17 maps resolve to the selected method/profile/recipe, meaningful diagrams, localized required text and safety; quantities and recipe attribution match reviewed evidence. A fallback cannot route an unsupported exact profile into a superficially similar generic method.
- **Physical event:** opening any screen starts no extraction timer. First-water/bloom share one origin; espresso includes pre-infusion; sealed AeroPress, post-fill Switch, coffee-addition Clever and refrigerated cold brew retain their selected origins. Clicking a reference clock never advances a physical action.
- **Isolation:** exercise read-ahead backward/forward/overview while an active pour and wait run. Session ID, recipe snapshot, stage, timestamps, actuals and confirmations remain identical except for elapsed time. Return takes one clear action.
- **Completion:** added vs cumulative water labels agree arithmetically; output is never called input. Manual/indicator completion works even outside advisory ranges. Espresso early preset stop records its actual boundary without restarting. Drainage and filtration never complete because an animation ended.
- **Lifecycle:** rotation, activity/process recreation, lock/unlock, background/foreground, notification tap, reboot, timezone change and wall-clock adjustment restore a truthful physical state. Prompt pause never freezes extraction. No duplicated alerts/logs on retries; notification denial still permits reliable foreground use with honest reminder availability.
- **Interaction:** TalkBack and Switch Access complete preparation, live action, pause/resume, reading and recovery. Test narrow 320–360 dp, 200% font scale, long translations, landscape, large displays and reduced motion. No clipped required text, obscured focused control, overlapping touch area or gesture-only action. Contrast measured in both themes.
- **At the counter:** users can name the next action/quantity/completion cue in a brief glance and return from reading without losing place. Observe kettle handling, wet hands, steam/glare, distance and one-handed taps on representative physical brewers. Never infer this outcome from browser screenshots.
- **Review boundary:** browser flow checks prove this prototype only. Native tests prove the Android implementation only within their executed scenarios. Physical safety, taste, hardware variation, localization quality and release readiness require their own evidence.
