# Automatic drip: let the brewer handle the pouring

Editorially reviewed, 2026-10-02. English research content; no physical brew test.

An automatic filter brewer heats reservoir water and distributes it over ground coffee. You choose the coffee, grind and batch; the machine handles water delivery. This guide starts with [Counter Culture's published automatic-brewer recipe](https://counterculturecoffee.com/pages/auto-drip), then gives separate Moccamaster instructions. Use the example only in a brewer whose manual permits a 1,000 g water batch and the corresponding coffee dose. Filter shape, minimum batch, controls and cleaning depend on the machine.

## Equipment and starting recipe

Have the brewer, its matching basket and paper, its carafe, a coffee scale, a clean filling jug, and a burr grinder or suitably ground coffee.

Use **63 g coffee and 1,000 g fresh drinking water**, measured separately. Start medium-coarse, a little finer than kosher salt. The exact mass ratio is 1:15.87; Counter Culture calls this approximately 1:16. The 1,000 g is **water entering the reservoir**, not a promised weight of coffee in the carafe. Grounds and the brewer retain water; measure output if you need to know it. No kettle temperature or manual bloom is required. The brewer controls heating, and this generic recipe has no universal finishing time.

## Make the coffee

1. **Prepare the machine while it is off.** Check its manual for the correct paper and allowed batch. Seat one filter without folded edges obstructing the basket. Complete any first-use water-only rinse the manufacturer requires. Rinse the paper only as the machine instructions permit, and empty that rinse water before brewing.
2. **Measure the reservoir water.** Weigh 1,000 g in a clean jug and transfer it into the reservoir. Stay below the machine's maximum line. Reservoir cup marks are volume labels; they are not gram marks. Use the water type recommended for your appliance.
3. **Prepare the coffee.** Grind 63 g and place it in the filter. Gently level the loose grounds; do not press them down. Leave room for the bed to swell.
4. **Seat the receiving vessel and start.** Position the basket, carafe and required lids correctly. Set a batch selector if your model has one, then start the brew. If timing for comparison, start the clock when you press the brew switch.
5. **Wait for delivery and drainage.** Follow the machine's completion indicator and let the basket finish draining. Keep the receiving vessel in place. An empty tank, a timer alarm or a power light changing is not sufficient if coffee is still flowing.
6. **Serve carefully.** Pour using the handle. Record taste and actual output if useful. Do not keep heating an empty glass carafe.

## Moccamaster variants

**KBGV Select:** use **#4 cone paper** and its glass carafe with lid. The [USA dosing table](https://support.moccamaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500009389881-How-much-coffee-should-I-use-in-my-Moccamaster) gives **55 g / 1,000 ml** or **28 g / 500 ml**. Its [quick guide](https://support.moccamaster.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/1500014339002) assigns the half-carafe selector to **4–6 manufacturer cups** and the full selector to **8–10**: use half for 500 ml and full for 1,000 ml. These controls alter the cycle; they do not measure the water for you. The table maps 4 cups to 500 ml and 8 to 1,000 ml, approximately 125 ml per labelled cup, rather than a household mug. Maximum reservoir capacity is 1.25 L. Expect roughly 4–6 minutes after starting, then confirm drainage. Heating remains automatic. The [European Select manual](https://www.moccamaster.eu/pub/media/handleidingen/talen/Manual_SELECT.pdf) instead recommends 60 g/L: **30 g / 500 ml** and **60 g / 1,000 ml** are that volume-based alternative, not exact mass conversions.

**Cup-One:** use **#1 cone paper**, **20 g coffee**, and cool water **to the actual marked reservoir level**. The [manufacturer's scanned Dutch manual, page 8](https://www.moccamaster.nl/pub/media/handleidingen/Gebruiksaanwijzing-Cup-One-2.0-zonder-wegwerpbeker.pdf) specifies 18–21 g at that mark and about four minutes; 20 g is our selected point in that range. Check its small basket outlet before brewing. Use a stable, compatible mug that comfortably holds the brew; the older manual specifies at least 300 ml. Wait until dripping finishes; Cup-One has no drip stop. Its machine-reported 92–96 °C is descriptive, not a user setting. Older instructions describe shutoff after transfer; the [current USA page](https://us.moccamaster.com/products/cup-one) lists delayed power-off. Finish by flow, not the switch. Do not substitute an assumed 300 g fill: the USA product lists 0.33 L / 10 oz, while its dosing chart lists 0.3 L / 10 oz. Those labels are not exact unit conversions or measured output.

## Taste, flow and cleanup

For overflow, stop the brewer using its normal control and let it cool before inspecting it. First check filter fit, basket outlet and receiving-vessel placement. On Cup-One, clear the small outlet with the supplied tool while off, unplugged and cool. If these are correct, try a slightly coarser grind next time. For bitter coffee, also check excessive hotplate holding; for weak coffee, confirm dose and water before grinding slightly finer. Change one thing per batch. Moccamaster recommends adding hot water to the served cup if its normal recipe is too strong.

Keep hands clear of steam, the outlet arm and hot glass. Do not remove Moccamaster lids, basket or receiving vessel during brewing. Switch off, unplug and cool before cleaning; never immerse the appliance. Hand-wash the basket and glass carafe. Follow the model's current descaling instructions and product rinse directions; [current USA guidance](https://support.moccamaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500009428621-How-frequently-should-I-descale-and-clean-my-brewer-and-what-should-I-use) says every 100 cycles, sooner with hard water, and advises against vinegar.
