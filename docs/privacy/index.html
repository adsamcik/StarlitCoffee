Privacy Policy

Effective date: 21 August 2026 · Applies to: the current release of Starlit Coffee for Android and this website

In short: Your coffee data stays on your device, and I cannot see it. Starlit Coffee has no accounts, ads, analytics, tracking, or automatic crash upload. A small amount of data leaves your device only when a feature needs it or when you choose to share it. Those cases are listed below.

1. About this policy

Starlit Coffee is developed and published by Adam Novak (GitHub: adsamcik) in the Czech Republic. This policy covers the Starlit Coffee Android app and this website. For privacy questions, email play@adsamcik.com.

The app has no developer-operated backend and does not create an account for you. I am the data controller only where data-protection law makes me responsible for a processing operation. Services such as Google, GitHub, Open Food Facts, and websites you choose to contact are responsible for their own processing under their privacy notices.

2. Data on your device

Starlit Coffee keeps the information you create in its private Android storage. This includes:

  • coffee bags, barcodes, photos, roasters, origins, roast dates, and notes;
  • brew logs, recipes, ratings, flavour tags, grinders, and presets;
  • unfinished coffee-label drafts, including staged photos, partial fields, and your edits;
  • your settings and recent scan history; and
  • local troubleshooting records, such as app logs, crash stacks, unresponsive-app events, and Android process-exit reasons.

I cannot remotely access, retrieve, change, or delete this data. Barcode and label recognition run on your device. Bundled Google ML Kit text recognition provides the baseline; if you enable enhanced recognition, the Mindlayer service installed on the same device can add details through a local bound service. Images and extracted text are not sent to an online AI service. Camera preview frames are discarded after analysis. Photos you capture or import for an editable draft remain in private, no-backup storage until you save or explicitly discard that draft.

Diagnostics are also local. They use limited storage, exclude text and unknown values by default, and have no automatic uploader. In Settings → Support & privacy → Diagnostics, you can change capture options, delete all diagnostic data, or review a support package before choosing where to share or save it.

The app does not request your name, email address, contacts, advertising ID, or device location. It does not include advertising, analytics, attribution, Firebase Analytics, or Crashlytics.

3. When data leaves your device

These are the only app and website features that send or expose data outside your device:

  • Barcode lookup. When the app recognizes a product barcode during a bag scan, it sends the barcode digits to Open Food Facts to request product details. Open Food Facts also receives the IP address needed for the connection and the app's User-Agent. It does not receive your photos, notes, coffee history, advertising ID, or device location. I do not receive the request.
  • QR website lookup. The app asks before requesting information from a website found in a bag's QR code. If you approve, that website receives its requested URL, your IP address, and the app's User-Agent. The app accepts only checked public HTTPS destinations. The website's own privacy policy applies.
  • Android backup. If device backup is enabled, Android may copy the app database and preferences to your private system backup, commonly managed by Google, and restore them later. Bag photos, pending scan work, and diagnostics are excluded. I cannot access your backup account.
  • Sharing and support. A brew card or reviewed diagnostic package leaves the app only after you choose a recipient or save location in Android. The recipient's privacy terms then apply. If you send a package to me, I receive that package and your accompanying message.
  • Website and email. This website has no analytics or advertising code added by me. GitHub Pages may process normal web request information such as your IP address, browser details, and requested page. If you email me, the email providers involved process your address, message, and attachments to deliver and store the conversation.

Relevant notices: Open Food Facts, Google, and GitHub. These services and recipients you choose may process data outside the European Economic Area under the transfer safeguards described in their notices.

4. Permissions

  • Camera: scans barcodes and bag labels while a scanner is active.
  • Notifications: shows brew timers, reminders, and progress for long-running on-device bag analysis if you allow notifications.
  • Mindlayer app visibility: checks only whether a supported Mindlayer service is installed. The app does not inspect or transmit your general installed-app list.

You can revoke camera or notification access in Android Settings. An Android permission is a device control, not consent to unrelated data use and not, by itself, GDPR consent.

5. Retention and deletion

  • Local records: remain until you delete them in the app or uninstall the app.
  • Unfinished coffee drafts: remain available until you save or explicitly discard them. Closing a draft removes its staged photos and structured fields; a minimal ownership marker prevents late background work from recreating it.
  • Diagnostics: can be deleted from the Diagnostics screen. They are also removed when you uninstall the app and are excluded from backup.
  • Android backup: may remain after uninstalling. Manage it through Android or your backup provider.
  • Email and support packages I receive: are kept only as long as needed to answer you, investigate the issue, protect the app and its users, or meet a legal obligation.
  • Outside services: keep data under their own retention rules. I cannot delete data held only by them.

6. GDPR and your rights

Why mention GDPR if the data is local? Because GDPR is about processing personal data, not just uploading it to a developer's server. Keeping data on your device greatly limits my access and the privacy risk, but it does not automatically settle who is legally responsible for every operation. Your own purely personal or household use does not give you business GDPR obligations.

In practice, I do not possess your local coffee records and cannot identify, retrieve, or delete them for you. GDPR remains relevant to processing for which I am legally responsible, including the limited network features and personal data you send me for support.

Where I am the controller, the legal bases are:

  • Article 6(1)(b): providing an app feature or support you request;
  • Article 6(1)(f): answering messages, investigating problems, and protecting the app and its users, where those interests do not override your rights; and
  • Article 6(1)(c): meeting a legal obligation.

For personal data I hold, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, or receive it, and to object to some processing. Email play@adsamcik.com. I will normally respond within one month and may ask only for information reasonably needed to verify your request. For data held only on your device, use the app's controls or uninstall the app. For data held by another service, contact that service.

I do not sell personal data or use it for advertising, profiling, data brokerage, or automated decisions about people. Starlit Coffee is a coffee-brewing tool and is not designed for children.

7. Security

Android provides app-private storage, permission controls, and backup protection. Starlit Coffee also minimizes stored data, uses HTTPS for network requests, checks QR destinations, excludes photos and diagnostics from backup, and requires your review before sharing diagnostics.

No software can be perfectly secure. Keep Android updated and use a secure screen lock. Anyone who can unlock or compromise your device may be able to reach data stored on it.

8. Changes, contact, and complaints

I may update this policy when the app changes. The effective date will change, and material changes will be announced in the app or release notes where appropriate. The edit history is public.

Contact play@adsamcik.com with a privacy question or request. You may also complain to the data-protection authority where you live or work, or where an issue occurred:

  • Czech Republic: Úřad pro ochranu osobních údajů (ÚOOÚ) — uoou.gov.cz.
  • United Kingdom: Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) — ico.org.uk.
  • EEA: list of national data-protection authorities.
